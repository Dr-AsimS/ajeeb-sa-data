
# Modeling and Optimal Control of Thermal Environment in Pig Houses

## Abstract and Key Findings

- The study introduces a **Thermal Ventilation Model (TVM)**, based on **enthalpy balance**, to control temperature and humidity in pig houses, specifically designed for regions like Guangdong, China, known for high temperatures and humidity.
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC)** is used to optimize ventilation rates, which improves both **energy efficiency** and **environmental comfort**.
- The model's accuracy is validated using **real-world data** from Guangdong, with the model showing high performance in **spring** and **summer**, though higher energy consumption is noted in extreme conditions.

## Thermal Environment Model (TVM)

The **TVM** integrates both **temperature** and **humidity** control for effective thermal management in pig houses.

### Key Components:
- **Metabolic Heat Production**: Heat generated by the pigs, which affects the indoor environment.
- **Ventilation Heat Dissipation**: The role of ventilation in removing heat and moisture from the building.
- **External Environmental Factors**: Solar radiation and outdoor temperature.

### Working Principle:
- **Enthalpy Balance**: The system uses enthalpy balance to control indoor climate by adjusting the airflow based on both temperature and humidity.
- **Dynamic Adjustment**: The ventilation system adjusts in real-time, considering external environmental conditions like outdoor temperature and humidity.

## Model Predictive Control (MPC)

**MPC** is an optimization strategy used for controlling the indoor environment by adjusting ventilation rates based on predictions.

### Objectives:
- Minimize deviations in **temperature** and **humidity**.
- Optimize **energy usage** by adjusting ventilation dynamically.

### MPC Approach:
- **State Vectors**: The system tracks temperature and humidity over time.
- **Dynamic Adjustments**: Ventilation rates are adjusted based on real-time data to maintain optimal conditions for the pigs.

## Key Contributions

- **TVM** provides integrated control of **temperature** and **humidity**, ensuring **animal welfare** and **productivity**.
- **MPC** optimizes energy consumption while maintaining comfort, outperforming traditional rule-based control systems.

## Results

### Model Performance:
- **Spring**: MPC maintained **100%** comfort temperature zone and **100%** comfort humidity zone.
- **Summer**: MPC maintained **83%** of the comfort temperature zone and **85%** of the humidity zone.

### Energy Efficiency:
- **Summer**: Higher energy consumption was observed with MPC (**91.2 kWh**) compared to traditional rule-based control (**80.8 kWh**).
- **Winter**: Minimal difference in energy consumption between MPC (**15.4 kWh**) and rule-based control (**15.2 kWh**).

## Control Settings and Comparisons

### MPC vs Rule-Based Control:
- **Spring**: MPC maintained comfort temperature for **100%** of the time, while rule-based control achieved **91%**.
- **Summer**: MPC achieved **83%** comfort in temperature and **85%** in humidity, whereas rule-based control achieved **43%** and **80%**, respectively.
- **Energy Consumption**: MPC had a slightly higher energy consumption, especially during summer.

## MPC Formulation

The optimization problem for **MPC** is described as:

```
min_u  ∑ from k=0 to N-1 ( λT * (T - Tref)² + λH * (RH - RHref)² + λE * u² )
```

### Key Parameters:
- **Comfort Temperature**: 21°C - 28°C (adjusted based on pig weight and stage).
- **Comfort Humidity**: 30% - 80%.
- **Ventilation Rate**: 4 - 120 m³/h.
- **Prediction Horizon**: 1 hour.
- **Control Horizon**: 10 minutes.

### Equation for Temperature and Humidity:
- **Temperature Update**:
  ```
  Tin(t+1) = Hin(t) - Din(t) * (2501 + 1.86 * Tin(t)) / (1.006 + Din(t) * 1.86)
  ```
- **Humidity Update**:
  ```
  RHin(t+1) = Din(t) / (0.622 * P / (es(t) / Din(t))) * 100
  ```

## Pig Building Description and Data Collection

### Building Features:
- **Centralized Ventilation**: Includes ceiling-mounted exhaust fans and underground tunnel-based inlet.
- **Air Quantity Control (AQC)**: Monitors real-time airflow to maintain optimal conditions.

### Data Collection:
- Temperature and humidity data were collected during three periods: **Winter**, **Spring**, and **Summer**.
- **Ventilation Rate**: Measured using AQC devices.

## Model Evaluation

### Performance Metrics:
- **RMSE (Root Mean Square Error)**: Measures the error between predicted and observed values.
- **MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)**: Measures relative error as a percentage.
- **R² (Coefficient of Determination)**: Indicates how well the model explains observed variability.

### Results:
- **Winter**: RMSE = **1.23°C**, MAPE = **8.6%**.
- **Spring**: RMSE = **0.81°C**, MAPE = **4.3%**.
- **Summer**: RMSE = **0.60°C**, MAPE = **1.5%**.

## Conclusion and Discussion

- The **TVM** and **MPC** systems were validated and found to be effective in regulating the thermal environment in pig housing.
- The **MPC** approach outperformed traditional rule-based control in terms of maintaining **comfort** and **energy efficiency**, though the energy consumption was higher during extreme conditions.
- Future work will aim to further refine the model for **extreme winter conditions** and integrate more **environmental factors** like wind and solar radiation.
